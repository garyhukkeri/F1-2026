Essentially identical brake usage — what changes corner-to-corner is *where* they brake and *how long* they brake for, driven primarily by the tyre compound (HARD vs SOFT).

## Corner-by-corner observations

- **T1 (Sainte Devote):** LEC brakes ~5 m earlier (95 m vs 84 m) but reaches a higher minimum speed (107 vs 105). HAM on softs brakes later & deeper.
- **T5 (Mirabeau Haute):** Both bottom out at 75 km/h. LEC's longer brake (80 m vs 92 m) is because HAM's SOFTs give more grip at higher speed → he stays on the gas longer.
- **T6 (Loews / Fairmont hairpin):** Slowest point of the lap. LEC 44 km/h, HAM 45 km/h. The classic Monaco minimum-speed zone.
- **T10/11 (Nouvelle Chicane):** Hardest brake-zone of the lap (~115-120 m). HAM enters at 272 km/h (DRS), LEC at 252 km/h.
- **T15-17 (Rascasse):** Multi-apex sequence. Both treat it as one long brake event from ~2570 m to ~2660 m.
- **T17 (Rascasse exit):** Tiny corrective brake on LEC (~8 m), barely registered on HAM — different lines through the corner.
- **T18 (Anthony Noghes):** Approach from start/finish straight. LEC enters 190 km/h, HAM 200 km/h.

## Race trend

Across green-flag laps (lap_time < 90 s), both Ferraris hold a tight 24-28 % brake-distance share. HAM trends slightly higher on average (~27 %) — consistent with softer tyres allowing later, harder braking. Late-race spike for HAM (>30 %) coincides with his final SOFT stint where he pushed hardest.

# Leclerc DNF — brake-degradation investigation

Dashboard sections 5-7 in `visualizations/monaco-gp-race.html`.
Data: `analysis/monaco_lec_brake_deg.json` (generated by `analysis/monaco_lec_brake_deg.py`).

**Important data limitation:** this dataset has **no brake-temperature or brake-wear channel**. The `brake`
telemetry is binary (pedal on/off). True thermal fade cannot be measured directly — only inferred from braking
*behaviour*.

## Method

Leclerc DNF'd on lap 65. To look for degradation we tracked, per green-flag lap, the two heaviest braking zones
(T1 Sainte Devote, T10 Nouvelle Chicane): brake point (m into lap), brake distance, brake duration, entry speed,
minimum speed. A failing brake would show the brake point creeping steadily earlier, brake distance/time growing,
and minimum speed rising.

## Findings — no progressive degradation in green running

57 green-flag laps analysed (laps 2-59):

| Zone | Brake-point drift | Brake dist (early→late) | Entry speed (early→late) | Min speed (early→late) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 (Sainte Devote) | **+0.13 m/lap** | 92 → 87 m | 247 → 247 | 103 → 107 |
| T10 (Nouvelle Chicane) | **−0.10 m/lap** | 106 → 110 m | 241 → 248 | 62 → 63 |

Both brake-point trends are effectively zero (a genuine fade would be several m/lap negative). Brake distances,
entry speeds and minimum speeds are flat to the end of green running. **No behavioural signature of brake
degradation is present.**

## The retirement (lap 65)

- Laps 61-65 ran under a **Safety Car** (slow laps, 95-124 s) — not representative of braking under load.
- On L65 the car never reached racing speed, slowed progressively, and came to a **final stop at ~2971 m**
  (just past the last corner, T19 Anthony Noghes, on the run to the line) **~113 s into the lap**.
- Telemetry then logged the car parked for the remainder (372 s total), with the brake reading "on" while stationary.

## Conclusion

The available telemetry **does not support a gradual brake-degradation story**. Leclerc's braking was stable and
strong right through lap 59. The DNF occurred at low speed under a Safety Car, consistent with a **sudden/terminal
failure or a precautionary stop** rather than progressive fade. 

Caveat: with no brake-temperature channel, a thermal problem that did not change the driver's pedal inputs (or one
that manifested only in the non-representative SC laps) would not be visible here.

## Were the brakes applied into the corner at the crash? (Dashboard section 8)

Direct question: at the moment Leclerc stopped/crashed on L65, were the brakes on, or does the data show no brake
applied? Examining the raw (full-rate) telemetry of the final approach to Turn 19:

- Brake comes ON at ~2924 m / 109.8 s approaching T19 and **stays on continuously** to the stop.
- **Brake pedal on for 100 % of the final 3 seconds**, and **still applied on the last moving sample** (brake = 1).
- Speed bleeds 98 → 88 → 75 → 65 → 58 → 50 → 31 → 12 → 0 km/h.
- Longitudinal deceleration peaks at **−14.8 m/s² (~1.51 g)** — beyond his normal ~−10 m/s² braking — and the
  distance freezes at exactly 2971.4 m and never moves again (abrupt, permanent stop).

**Answer: the brakes were definitively applied into the corner.** This is the opposite of a "no brake applied"
scenario — he was hard on the brakes the entire way in, and the brake was still on when the car came to rest. The
sharp deceleration spike + instant permanent stop is consistent with an impact while braking, not a coasting/no-brake
stop.

## Corroboration with Leclerc's radio (Dashboard section 9)

Radio: *"This brakes! ... I put the least amount of brakes I could possibly do ... the rear brakes were not working
at all."*

The binary `brake` channel only proves the pedal was pressed — it can't measure force. The longitudinal
accelerometer (`acc_x`) measures the deceleration **actually achieved**, which is the real test of whether the
brakes worked.

Per-lap median braking deceleration (while on the pedal, speed > 20 km/h):

- **Laps 2-59 (healthy):** −10 to −12 m/s² (median −10.7).
- **Laps 60-65 (failure):** −4.4 to −6.3 m/s² (median −4.9) — **~54 % weaker**.

The collapse is **sudden at lap 60**, not gradual — matching a mechanical failure rather than wear. Simultaneously
his brake-on sample count nearly doubled (~80 → 140-170 per lap): he was on the pedal far *more* while achieving far
*less* retardation. This is the quantitative fingerprint of failed brakes and directly corroborates Leclerc's account.

**Full reconstructed sequence:**
1. Laps 2-59: braking rock-steady, full deceleration, zero degradation signature.
2. Lap 60: braking force suddenly collapses ~54 %. Failure onset.
3. Laps 60-64: nurses the car with ~half braking force, on the brakes nearly twice as much, trying to compensate.
4. Lap 65, Turn 19: applies the "least amount of brakes" he can; with the rear brakes dead the car won't stop
   properly; comes to rest / impacts at 2971 m with the pedal still pressed.

The earlier "no degradation" finding (sections 5-6) and this finding are consistent: there was **no gradual fade** —
the brakes were fine until a **sudden failure at lap 60**.
